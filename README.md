# Indonesia's Energy Transition
 
A research visualization examining Indonesia's per capita energy consumption from 1984 to 2024, with a focus on the country's progress toward its renewable energy targets.
 
**[View The Report](https://ak7647a.github.io/Portfolio-Problem-Set-2/)** 

 
## Research Topic
 
Indonesia's energy consumption has grown dramatically over the past four decades, rising from roughly 2,500 kWh per capita in 1984 to over 10,000 kWh in 2024. Rather than diversifying its energy mix as demand rose, Indonesia became increasingly reliant on fossil fuels, especially coal. This project asks: **how far is Indonesia from meeting its own climate commitments, and what does the data say about the pace of its renewable transition?**
 
---
 
## Data
 
- **Source:** [Our World in Data — Per Capita Energy Consumption](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/per-capita-energy-stacked?country=~IDN&overlay=sources)
- **Coverage:** Indonesia, 1984–2024
- **Variables:** Per capita energy consumption (kWh) by source — coal, oil, gas, solar, wind, biomass, geothermal, and other renewables
- **Format:** Downloaded as `.csv` and cleaned in R
---
 
## Approach
 
The analysis is split into two visualizations:
 
1. **Long-run energy mix (1984–2024)** — a stacked area chart showing how Indonesia's energy sources have evolved over 40 years, highlighting coal's dominance and oil's plateau around 2004.
2. **Renewable zoom-in (2018–2024)** — a focused view of recent renewable progress, revealing that solar and wind together account for under 20 kWh per person which is less than 0.2% of total consumption. However, with a promising future in geothermal, this massive ednowment is slowing being explored.
These are read alongside Indonesia's stated policy targets of 23% renewables by 2030 (already delayed from 2025) and net zero by 2060. The data reveals a significant implementation gap, where renewable capacity must outpace a rising baseline instead of just substituting existing fossil fuel use.
